# Design

## Context

`runClusterSummaryJob` reads a cluster's fact ids (`readClusterFactIds`), decrypts them (`readSummaryFacts`, each with a `groupKey`: the root entity or `subject:<text>`), chooses at most 60 with `selectSummaryFacts` (round robin over groups, biggest first, preferring a page not yet used), and sends `buildSummaryMessages` lines `F<n> | subject | attribute | value unit | "quote"` with `clusterSummary@1`. Answers are validated, statements citing no valid fact are dropped, the rest are stored encrypted. `readSummariesView` shows clusters, each with statements and resolved citations.

The comparison table already shows every product's cells, but only on its own tab; the Summaries tab has no structured view. `improve-fact-quality` added a bundled attribute table (`getAttributeKey`, `ATTRIBUTE_SYNONYMS`) and facts made from a page's product data (`productData@1`: price, brand, availability, rating).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- A shopper sees, per product, name, price, key specs and source, reliably, with no model.
- The model's statements are about named products, comparing them when there are several.
- The 60 facts the model sees are the ones that matter.

**Non-Goals:**

- Splitting a cluster into product groups or separating accessories (research clustering).
- Gaps, source kinds, or UI noise.
- Guaranteeing a good statement from a 4B model: the cards carry the useful part even when the statements are poor.

## Decisions

### 1. Cards are computed when summaries are read, not stored

`readProductCards(facts, details)` is a pure function over the cluster's decrypted facts (already read for citations), so there is no storage, no staleness rule and no job: change a fact, reject a fact, and the card changes. A cluster's facts are read once per view (the readers already decrypt each fact for citations; the card reader reads all the cluster's facts once, at most a few hundred).

### 2. Which entities get a card

Group the cluster's facts by root entity (`groupKey` of an entity, as `readSummaryFacts` does). An entity gets a card when its type is `product` and it has at least 3 facts, after rejected facts are removed. Cards are ordered by fact count, then name, and at most 6 are shown with "+N more products" after them. Facts of entity type `unknown` or other types make no card; they stay in the statements and the table.

### 3. The price, and the key specs

The price is the value of the entity's facts with attribute key `price` (by `getAttributeKey`), taking the product-data fact (`productData@1`) when there is one, else the value most facts state. Key specs: group the entity's facts by `getAttributeKey`, drop `price`, `brand`, `availability`, `rating` and generic attributes, order the groups by the bundled importance order `KEY_SPEC_ORDER` (screen size, display, battery capacity, storage, ram, main camera, processor, charging, weight, water resistance, then the rest by fact count), keep the first 5 and, within a group, show the value with the most facts, the first stated winning a tie. A variant attribute (storage, colour) shows its values together, as the table does. Values are shown as the page wrote them; no conversion.

### 4. Sources on a card

The price carries the site and day of its page (`readPageSourceDetails`), shown like a citation. Each shown value links to its quote through the same `SummaryCitation` shape and its "Wrong" control, so a card is as checkable as a statement. A fact the user rejected never appears.

### 5. Importance scoring replaces spread

`selectSummaryFacts` keeps its signature and returns the same shape but scores each fact: 100 for product-data facts, 60 for an attribute in `KEY_SPEC_ORDER` (higher the earlier in the order), 20 for a fact whose attribute and value another page also states, 0 otherwise; ties by fact id. At most one fact per (entity, attribute key) is considered before any second one; then the highest scores fill the 60, with the spread rule as the tie-breaker so no product is left out while another has ten. Facts left out are counted as before (`omittedFactCount`).

### 6. The model sees the product's name

`readSummaryFacts` adds `entityName`: the root entity's decrypted name, else the subject. `buildSummaryMessages` writes it as the second column. The raw subject goes with the quote only. A fact's `groupKey` is unchanged.

### 7. `clusterSummary@2` and statement filter

New prompt files beside `@1`: each claim must name the product it is about as the facts give it; "the device", "it" and "this product" are forbidden; when two or more products are present, the first statement is a short comparison (price, then one or two specs several have); at most 8 statements, citations required as before. The version is recorded on the summary, so a summary written with `@1` shows as before and is regenerated by "Refresh". In the view, a statement that contains none of the cluster's product names (compared normalised, as `normalizeEntityName` does; a statement names a product when it holds the product's name or two words of it in a row — a shop's name for a product is long, and a claim says `Pixel 10a` — or the whole name when it is a single word) is left out when at least one other statement does; if none do, all are shown, so nothing is hidden when naming simply failed. The filter is applied when reading, never to what is stored.

### 8. The attribute table moves to `shared`

`getAttributeKey`, `isVariantAttributeKey` and `ATTRIBUTE_SYNONYMS` are now used by two features. Per the layering rule they move to `@/shared/getAttributeKey` (with the table in its `constants.ts`) and `comparisonTable` imports them from there; behaviour and specs are unchanged. `KEY_SPEC_ORDER` lives in `clusterSummaries`, since only cards use it.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **A cluster that mixes phones and straps** yields cards for both, because clustering is not split yet. A strap is a `product` too. Accepted; the next change on clustering fixes the cause.
- **Entity type from a 4B model is unreliable.** A product typed `unknown` gets no card. The product-data facts are typed `product` by us, so a shop page's main product is always typed right, which is what matters most.
- **`KEY_SPEC_ORDER` is shaped by phones and watches.** For other products everything falls back to fact count. It is data, easy to extend.
- **A statement filter can hide a good statement** that names the product by a variant of its name. It applies only when another statement names one, and hides only at read time.
- **Cards duplicate the comparison table's data.** They answer a different question (one product at a glance); the cost is one reader.

## Migration Plan

None stored. `clusterSummary@1` summaries keep showing and are replaced on Refresh. Rolling back removes the cards and leaves summaries as they were.

## Open Questions

- Should the cards show the product's rating and availability when known? Left out until the page is seen; they are product-data facts and are easy to add.
- Should a card be hidden when the cluster is a single product? Shown anyway: one card is still the useful summary.
